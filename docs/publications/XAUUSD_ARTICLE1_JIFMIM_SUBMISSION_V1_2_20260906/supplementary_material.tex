\section*{S1. Registry construction and status labels}

The registry was frozen before consolidated specification outcome computation. Every row identifies the source block, source candidate, economic family, analysis role, direction, horizon, reference interval, alignment and cross-feed requirements, source-code digest, configuration digest, and serialized signal contract. Exact file and registry digests are stored in the accompanying machine-readable manifest.

The 39 rows labeled \texttt{PRIMARY\_LOCKED\_RERUN} form the article's primary universe. The 8 machine-learning rows are labeled \texttt{HISTORICAL\_FORENSIC\_CASE\_STUDY\_ONLY}; they were historically selected and had been exposed to later diagnostic information. They were intentionally not rerun and do not enter any primary result.

\section*{S2. Data contract and source bindings}

The primary intraday contract is 2015-01-02 07:00 UTC through, but not including, 2025-01-01 00:00 UTC. Cross-asset fixed and event-response blocks begin on 2016-01-01. The relative-value rule begins on 2012-01-01 and requires a 252-session formation sample. The monthly trend rule begins after a 12-month return lookback and a minimum of 120 past daily returns.

AMarkets server timestamps are converted under \texttt{EU\_DST\_GMT\_OFFSET\_PAIR}: subtract 120 minutes during standard time and 180 minutes during European daylight-saving time. The data-contract preflight recorded zero duplicate timestamps and zero invalid OHLC rows. The AMarkets alignment database, Dukascopy extended history, timestamp contract, official-event timestamps, and all additional inputs are identified by file digest in the frozen main-run manifest. Raw provider data are not included in this journal package.

\section*{S3. Exact strategy-class contracts}

\subsection*{S3.1. Successor price-only rules}

Nineteen non-overlapping rules cover ten families: trend continuation, trend pullback, range breakout, volatility expansion, session breakout, post-event momentum, post-event breakout, failed-breakout reversion, low-volatility range reversion, and $z$-score reversion. The registry supplies each fixed direction, horizon, threshold, and signal contract. The reference interval is 2015-01-02 07:00 UTC to 2025-01-01 00:00 UTC exclusive.

\subsection*{S3.2. Cross-asset fixed rules}

The 12 rules form six economic families with 4- or 12-hour horizons. Decisions occur during 06:00--19:00 UTC on weekdays and exclude official-event blackout periods. Standardized features use a 1,440-row rolling window and at least 480 past observations. Signals within a candidate are non-overlapping.

\subsection*{S3.3. Event-response rules}

The six rules use 4- or 12-hour horizons and enter only when an official event occurred 15--120 minutes before the decision. The official-event file is hash-bound. Standardized 15-minute reactions in XAUUSD, XAGUSD, and the dollar composite determine the locked directional conditions.

\subsection*{S3.4. Time-series momentum}

The sign of the prior 12 completed monthly returns determines a long or short position for one month. Volatility is estimated from past daily returns using a 60-day exponentially weighted center of mass and 261-day annualization. The annual volatility target is 20\%, absolute position is capped at 2.0, and turnover is charged on the complete monthly position change. The comparison benchmark is passive volatility-scaled long gold under the same risk and cost model.

\subsection*{S3.5. Gold-silver relative value}

The rule estimates $\log(XAU)=\alpha+\beta\log(XAG)$ over 252 completed daily closes. It enters at the next synchronized open after $|z|\ge2$, exits at the next open following a mean crossing, adverse $|z|\ge4$, or 60-day maximum holding, and uses gross-normalized fixed entry weights. The rule is not a parameter scan; structural-break and cointegration quantities are diagnostic only.

\section*{S4. Cost formulas and source-native gates}

For each directional trade, gross basis-point return is $10{,}000s(P_{out}/P_{in}-1)$. Source-specific net returns subtract the applicable round-trip or turnover cost. Severe costs are primary. Fixed stress is a secondary comparison: 10 bps for one-leg intraday rules and each unit of monthly turnover, and 20 bps for the two-leg rule.

\section*{S9. Deflated Sharpe ratios and PBO status}

The DSR uses a common daily calendar from 2016-01-01 through 2024-12-31, 365-day annualization, 39 eligible trials, and a 0.95 reporting threshold. The expected maximum daily Sharpe is 0.0477359441. No specification passes.

\input{supp_dsr_table}

PBO is not estimable because the universe does not form a valid combinatorially symmetric cross-validation matrix: event-only, intraday, monthly, and two-leg strategies have heterogeneous calendars, holding horizons, and objectives. No substitute scalar was computed after the outcome.

\section*{S10. Protocol deviations}

\begin{description}
\item[A1-DEV-001.] The redacted evidence copy of the canonical trend script had a different digest. Independent package evidence established that three malformed redaction placeholders in authorization and decision-reporting statements had been restored. No signal-generation or performance-computation statement changed. The registry was reissued as version 1.1 before the consolidated rerun.
\item[A1-DEV-002.] The first unified run requested the Dukascopy H1 table from the AMarkets SQLite file and aborted before entering any specification-outcome loop. Version 1.1 routed the AMarkets and Dukascopy tables to their already hash-bound databases and added schema gates. No strategy, cost, statistic, or seed changed. The aborted directory was retained.
\end{description}

\section*{S11. Independent audit boundary}

The independent audit verified safe archive paths, no duplicate members, CRC integrity, manifest completeness, file byte sizes and hashes, lock hashes, registry identities, outcome-table uniqueness, reference boundaries, finite returns, and the 28,685-row count. It independently recomputed each specification's count, four mean-return layers, severe-cost profit factor, all family max-$t$ results, and all DSR probabilities.

The audit did not regenerate every signal and trade from raw broker/vendor databases. It is therefore an output-level statistical reproduction with strong provenance checks, not a complete independent raw-data replication.
